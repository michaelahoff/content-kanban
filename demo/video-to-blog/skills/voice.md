# Voice

The blog explains one talk to a developer who has not seen it. It sounds like a careful colleague's write-up: plain, specific, and clear about who said what.

## Point of view

- Avoid "we", "our" and "us". The blog is not the speaker's team and not a club. Write about the talk ("Zhang argues…", "the talk's second rule") and to the reader ("you").
- Attribute every claim from the talk to the speaker by name. Keep the post's own additions visibly separate: "An illustration, not from the talk:".

## Sentences and paragraphs

- Short paragraphs: one idea, one to four sentences.
- Concrete beats general. Every section carries at least one specific: a number, an example, a tool or product name, a quote, a line of code.
- No hype and no filler. Do not use: game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, supercharge, seamless, harness, leverage (as a verb), delve, deep dive, landscape, "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting". No exclamation marks, no rhetorical questions as openers.
- Numbers as numerals; code, commands and identifiers in `backticks`.

## Headings

- Headings state claims, in sentence case: "Most tasks don't need an agent", not "When to use agents".
- H2 for the post's main claims (4–6 of them), H3 for supporting points. No H4.

## Terms

- Define a term once, in one sentence, where it first appears ("A *workflow* is a fixed sequence of model calls written in code."). Then use exactly that term; do not alternate synonyms.

## Citing the video

- When Transcript has `[m:ss]` times, follow a claim from the talk with a timestamp link: `[(4:12)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIDEO_ID&t=252s)`, where `t` is the time in seconds.
- Quote only words that are in Transcript. You may drop fillers ("uh", "um", "you know") and fix an obvious mishearing ("cause and latency" → "cost and latency"); never paraphrase inside quotation marks.

## Shape

- The first paragraph states the post's main point and who it is for. Never "In this post, we will…".
- Callouts, at most 3: `> **Note:**`, `> **Watch out:**` or `> **Try this:**`, each one or two sentences.
- End with "What to do with this": 3–5 concrete actions a reader can take this week. No recap of every section.
