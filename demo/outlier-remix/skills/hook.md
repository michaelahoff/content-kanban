# The seven-part hook

The first 30 seconds decide whether a click becomes a view. The viewer arrives holding the question the title and thumbnail planted. The hook confirms they are in the right place, raises the stakes, and moves into the content before they think about leaving.

## The parts, in order

| # | Part | Its job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pattern interrupt | A surprising, visual or very specific first line that matches the thumbnail | 1 sentence |
| 2 | Mirror the pain | The viewer's situation in their own words, so they think "that's me" | 1–2 sentences |
| 3 | The opportunity | A better way exists; say what it is, plainly | 1 sentence |
| 4 | Stakes | What staying the same costs: time, money, standing | 1 sentence |
| 5 | Concrete transformation | What they will have by the end of this video, something they can point to | 1 sentence |
| 6 | One credibility point | Why listen to you: one real, specific fact | 1 sentence |
| 7 | Direct transition | Straight into the first step; no recap, no "before we start" | 1 short sentence |

## Rules

- Spoken English: first person, contractions, sentences under about 15 words.
- The first line pays off the thumbnail. If the thumbnail shows a number, the first line is about that number.
- Nothing before part 7 that is not one of the parts: no greeting, channel name, "in this video", subscribe request or sponsor.
- Part 6 must be true, taken from the card, the notes or Brand assets. Otherwise write `[CREDIBILITY: …]` describing the proof that belongs there.
- Borrow the outlier's opening move (result first, a question, a bold claim), never its sentences.

## Worked example

Title: "From 212 Unread to 9: My AI Inbox Setup". Thumbnail: a red "212" struck through, a green "9" beside it.

> (1) This number used to be 212. This morning it was nine, and I didn't open my inbox once. (2) If email is the first thing you check and the last thing you finish, you know the feeling: you're always behind, and every unread is someone waiting on you. (3) The fix isn't more filters. It's an assistant that reads every message and only shows you the ones that need you. (4) Without it, that's about five hours a week of sorting you never get back. (5) By the end of this video you'll have the same setup running on your own inbox, with my rules to copy. (6) I've run it on three inboxes for eight months, including a shared support address. (7) Here's rule one.

120 words, about 40 seconds at a brisk pace. Why it works: (1) is the thumbnail's number said aloud; (2) uses the viewer's words, not the creator's; (4) puts a price on waiting; (5) promises a thing they will have, not a feeling; (6) is one specific fact (in a real intro, the creator's own); (7) wastes no time.

## Alternate first lines

Give three, each from a different angle:

- **Visual:** "Watch this counter."
- **Contrarian:** "Email filters are why you're drowning in email."
- **Result-first:** "Nine unread. That's my whole inbox, and I sorted none of it."
