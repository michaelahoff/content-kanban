# Retention: script structure and pacing

People leave a video at three moments: when the opening does not match the click, when a section stops promising anything new, and when the video signals that it is ending. The structure below answers each.

## Shape

1. **Cold open:** the intro, word for word: hook, stakes and transformation in 30–45 seconds.
2. **Roadmap, one line:** "Three parts: X, Y, and Z, the one most people get wrong." No more than three items, and tease the best one.
3. **Chapters:** 3–6 of them, each 1.5–3 minutes (225–450 words).
   - **Open a loop** in the first two sentences: a question, a promised reveal, "but there's a catch".
   - **Deliver** one idea, shown rather than told: a demo, an example, a before and after.
   - **Pay the loop off**, then bridge to the next with a new loop: "That handles X. It breaks the moment Y happens."
4. **The big loop:** the best item from the roadmap stays open across chapters and pays off at 60–75% of the running time.
5. **Ending:** the last payoff, one sentence on what the viewer can now do, then the next video.

## Re-hooks every 60–90 seconds (150–225 words)

Use a different kind each time:

- **Tease:** "The third rule saved me the most time. We'll get there."
- **Stakes:** "Skip this step and everything after it breaks."
- **Surprise:** "This is the part I got wrong for a year."
- **Question to the viewer:** "Have you ever had an assistant confidently do the wrong thing?"
- **Visual reset:** a new screen, a zoom, a cut to the result.

## Pattern interrupts

Something changes on screen every 15–20 seconds of speech. In the script that means a cue line at least every 50 words:

- `[B-ROLL: what we see]` for footage, screen recordings and cutaways.
- `[ON SCREEN: "exact text"]` for captions, numbers, lists and callouts.

## Call to action

- None in the first two minutes.
- One soft CTA straight after the first big payoff, 35–45% of the way through, tied to the value just delivered, 15 words at most: "If that rule saved you an hour, subscribe. Chapter four is better."
- None at the end. The end belongs to the next video.

## Ending

- Never announce the end ("that's it", "to wrap up", "thanks for watching"); viewers leave on those words, before the end screen.
- Last 15–20 seconds: name one problem this video did not solve and a video that does, then `[END SCREEN: next video]`. Example: "The one thing this setup can't do is reply for you. This video shows how. [END SCREEN: next video]"

## Pacing targets

- Time at 150 words a minute.
- Average sentence 15 words or fewer; no paragraph longer than 60 words.
- Every abstract claim gets a concrete example within two sentences.
- Chapter titles are claims or outcomes ("Rule 2: the AI never hits send"), not labels ("Configuration").
- Cut filler: "basically", "so yeah", "as I mentioned", "without further ado".

## Format

```
## [00:00] Intro
<the intro, verbatim>

Three parts: …

## [00:52] Rule 1: nothing reaches you twice
[ON SCREEN: "212 → 9"]
<spoken text>
[B-ROLL: the inbox sorting itself, sped up]
<spoken text>
```

## Check before you return

- Every chapter opens a loop and closes it.
- Re-hooks: at least one per 1.5 minutes of running time.
- One CTA, placed at 35–45%.
- The word count is within the target.
- The last line points to a next video.
