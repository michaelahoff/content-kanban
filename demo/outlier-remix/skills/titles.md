# Titles from an outlier

A title has one job: make the right viewer click when they see it beside the thumbnail, then let the video keep the promise. The outlier shows which shape of promise already works for this audience. We reuse the shape and write our own words.

## Hard rules

- **60 characters at most**, counted with a tool. Put the words that carry the promise in the first 40; phones cut the rest.
- **True.** Every number, result and claim is one the video delivers. A click the video does not pay off costs more in retention than it earns.
- **Ours.** Topic and tool names are free; the outlier's wording is not. No run of 4+ consecutive words from its title, and no one-word swaps ("4 Layers" → "5 Layers" is still theirs).
- **Clean.** Title Case or sentence case, at most one word in capitals, no emoji, no "!!!" or "???".
- **Split the job with the thumbnail.** The title says what; the thumbnail makes you feel why. Never plan the same words for both.

## The seven patterns

Write at least one of each, and at least two Mirrors. The examples below answer a made-up outlier, **"I Let AI Answer My Email for 30 Days"** (skeleton: `I Let [AI] [do a chore] for [time]`), for a video about an AI inbox assistant.

| Pattern | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror | The outlier's skeleton, our own slots and a twist | I Let AI Sort 4,000 Emails. It Got 3 Wrong |
| Number | A countable, finite promise | 3 Inbox Rules My AI Assistant Never Breaks |
| Contrast | Rejects the usual advice | Stop Writing Email Filters. Hire a Reader. |
| How I | Personal proof of a method | How I Reach Inbox Zero Without Opening Gmail |
| Question | A doubt the viewer already has | Can an AI Assistant Handle Your Email Safely? |
| Specific result | A measurable before and after | From 212 Unread to 9: My AI Inbox Setup |
| Named thing | A method with a name worth remembering | The Two-Folder Inbox: AI Triage in 20 Minutes |

## Scoring, 1 to 5 each

- **Curiosity (C):** 1, nothing unknown; 3, a mild "how?"; 5, a specific gap the viewer must click to close (a result without its method, a surprise, a tension).
- **Clarity (L):** 1, the topic is unclear; 3, topic clear, payoff vague; 5, a stranger knows in one second what they will get.
- **Specificity (S):** 1, abstract nouns; 3, one concrete detail; 5, numbers, named things, a timeframe or a before and after.
- **Thumbnail fit (T):** 1, it needs the thumbnail to repeat it; 3, it stands alone; 5, it implies a striking image the thumbnail can show without repeating a word.

16 out of 20 or better is ready to ship. The recommended title has the highest total; ties go to Clarity.

## Good and bad, with reasons

- ✗ **I Let AI Answer My Email for 31 Days.** The outlier's title with one number changed: copying.
- ✗ **AI Email Automation: The Ultimate 2026 Guide.** A search label, not a reason to click: no person, no gap, no result. C1 L5 S2 T1.
- ✗ **This AI Deleted My Boss's Email (SHOCKING).** A lie unless it happened, and the video never pays it off.
- ✗ **How I Used a Multi-Agent LLM Pipeline With IMAP Hooks to Triage Mail.** 68 characters, jargon first, cut off on phones.
- ✓ **From 212 Unread to 9: My AI Inbox Setup.** 39 characters; a before and after a stranger understands at once; the thumbnail can show the counter falling. C4 L5 S5 T5 = 19, if the numbers are real.
- ✓ **I Let AI Sort 4,000 Emails. It Got 3 Wrong.** Keeps the outlier's experiment shape but adds the tension it lacked: what went wrong. Shares only "I Let AI" with it. C5 L4 S5 T4 = 18.
